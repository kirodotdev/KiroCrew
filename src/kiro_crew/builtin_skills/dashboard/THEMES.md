# Themes to borrow

Whole-page looks an agent may pick when a page's style is left open, or
offer when the person asks what is possible. **Every one is optional.** Pick one that fits the
work, mix two, or invent your own. `GALLERY.md` shows how a page binds its
data; this file only suggests how it can look.

Each idea names three parts most pages have: **what waits** on the person
(asks, approvals, open items), **the day** (meetings, schedule, deadlines)
and **what feeds the page** (sources, workers, connections). Map them to
what this page actually reads, and drop a part the page does not have.

The rules in `SKILL.md` hold for every theme. Every value stays bound to its
fold and every link keeps working; only the look changes. Decoration never
stands in for data: a fish's size shows a real wait time, or it is just a
fish. Moving themes follow the motion and reduced-motion rules, and type
stays on the generic families.

## Retro tech

1. **8-bit arcade.** Pixel type and a chunky palette. What waits is a
   high-score table with wait time as the score. The day is levels on a
   world map, what feeds the page is power-up lights, and a blinking
   INSERT COIN shows when nothing needs the person.
2. **CRT terminal.** Green phosphor on a curved screen, with scanlines and a
   slight glow. What waits prints as terminal lines under a blinking cursor,
   the day is a cron-style schedule, and a failed source is a red ERR line.
3. **90s desktop.** Beveled grey panels everywhere. What waits sits in a
   mail-client pane, the day opens from a taskbar clock, and a "Needs you"
   dialog box carries an OK button.
4. **Early black-and-white desktop.** A file window lists what waits as
   files, the day sits in a small accessory window, and a smiling computer
   icon frowns when a source is down.

## Screen and story

5. **80s movie poster.** Neon pinks and purples on a sunset grid, chrome
   title type and tape tracking lines. A NOW PLAYING marquee holds the
   current meeting, and what waits rolls like the credits.
6. **1960s mission control.** Rows of consoles with amber readouts, a toggle
   light per source, a countdown to the next event, and what waits listed as
   controller callouts.
7. **Heist planning wall.** A blueprint background with what waits on index
   cards, red string joining items from the same person, and the day as the
   timing of the job.
8. **Starship console.** Rounded orange and lilac bars. What waits arrives as
   incoming hails, sources are ship systems, and a red alert shows when
   something needs the person.

## Things on a desk

9. **Cork board.** Cork texture, with what waits pinned as notes and index
   cards at slight angles. The day is a paper planner page, source health is
   coloured thumbtacks, and older notes yellow and curl.
10. **1950s general store.** Wood shelves, a hand-lettered price tag per
    item, a brass register for the totals, and a chalkboard of today's
    specials for the day.
11. **Diner order rail.** What waits hangs as tickets on a steel rail, older
    tickets get stained, the day is the specials board, and a bell flashes
    when a new item arrives.
12. **Library card catalog.** Typed cards in wooden drawers, grouped by
    person. The day is a due-date card, and sources are brass label plates.
13. **Typewriter desk from above.** What waits is typed on a page in the
    typewriter, beside a leather planner, a coffee ring and a NEEDS YOU ink
    stamp.

## Calm

14. **Bonsai garden.** Each waiting item is a leaf that turns green to amber
    to red as it waits, and falls when handled. Raked sand holds the day, and
    stones stand for sources.
15. **Aquarium.** Each waiting item is a fish whose size shows its wait. The
    day drifts by as bubbles at their times, and a sick fish appears when a
    source is down.
16. **Night sky.** What waits is stars, the oldest brightest. The day is
    constellations along a timeline, and a telescope readout shows details.
17. **Weather report.** The day's load becomes a forecast, what waits is a
    pressure front, sources are weather stations, and a storm warning shows
    when too much waits.

## Places

18. **Isometric city.** Each person asking is a building as tall as their
    count. The day is traffic along a road, and a broken source is a building
    with its lights out. Day and night follow the person's local time.
19. **Split-flap departure board.** What waits are departures: the platform
    is the source and the delay is the wait. The day is the next trains, and
    the flaps flip when a value changes.
20. **Pinball backglass.** What waits are bumpers, totals sit on score
    reels, sources are rollover lights, and TILT shows when a source fails.
21. **Steamship bridge.** Brass gauges for counts, a ship's log for what
    waits, a sea chart for the day's route, and a telegraph dial for status.
22. **1920s newspaper.** The biggest item is the headline and the rest are
    columns. The day is "Today's Events", and source status sits in the
    weather box.
